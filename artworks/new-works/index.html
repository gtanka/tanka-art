New Works (The Shore)

I am interested in the place where I live, and in our place on Earth in general.

There is a small beach between Jaffa and Tel Aviv.

To the right, the coastline stretches towards Tel Aviv — once a growing, cheerful city, now, after the recent war, as if covered in sand.

To the left rises the hill of the old port of Jaffa, with its history of endless wars and destruction. Today it is a beautiful park, a place of leisure, with restaurants and small galleries.

Between them lies a surfers' beach, without breakwaters.

In summer, the beach changes with the tides. In winter, the sea almost swallows it.

Winter also brings storms from the sea. Enormous clouds gather, and far away, beneath their strange shapes, the horizon becomes blurred. It is raining out at sea, and it is time to hurry home.

The clouds are so large. From an airplane, they look like pieces of cotton wool. They float above the Earth, suspended in the gases we call air. This layer of air extends only a relatively short distance above us. We were born and grew up inside it. Without it, we could not exist.

In the evenings, I walk to the beach to watch the sun set into the sea.

The Earth turns another side towards the Sun, and another day of my life comes to an end.

Of course, one could simply live without measuring time. I used to do that, for a little while. Now it seems completely impossible.

And so the glowing red or yellow sphere illuminates the clouds and slowly sinks into the greyish water, sometimes leaving an orange path across its surface.

Quite a few people gather along the promenade. They stop, stand still, and grow quiet as the sun goes down. Some wander along the water's edge, surrounded by orange and crimson reflections.

After travelling through England, I felt a desire to tell a story about the place where I live, about what is close to me.

I have lived near this beach for twenty-five years. I have come here at different times in my life.

The sea has always filled me with wonder and drawn me towards the water.
